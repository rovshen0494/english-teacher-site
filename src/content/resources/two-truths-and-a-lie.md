## Learning Objective

Students form clear statements about themselves and ask follow-up questions to investigate their classmates' claims.

## Materials

None needed, though notecards can help students plan their three statements first.

## Preparation

Give students a few minutes of quiet time to think of and write three statements about themselves — two true, one false.

## How It Works

1. Model the activity yourself first: share three statements about yourself and let students guess which is the lie.
2. Give students a few minutes to prepare their own three statements individually.
3. In small groups, each student shares their three statements.
4. The rest of the group asks follow-up questions before voting on which statement they think is false.
5. The student reveals the truth, and the next person takes their turn.

## Student Instructions

Think of three sentences about yourself — two true, one false. Can your group guess the lie?

## Variations

**Easier:** Allow simpler statements with a provided sentence structure, e.g. "I have..." / "I can..." / "I went to..."

**Harder:** Require the group to ask at least three follow-up questions before voting.

## Extension Activity

As a whole class, share the most surprising true statement that came up in each group.

## Teacher Tip

This is an excellent first-lesson activity for a new class — it naturally produces personal, memorable information that helps you learn about your students quickly.
