## Explanation

Certain mistakes appear repeatedly across candidates at similar levels and quietly cap band scores even when ideas are strong. In Speaking, this often means memorised answers that sound unnatural, or answers that are too short. In Writing, it's frequently a missing clear position in Task 2, or an essay that lists ideas without linking or developing them. Naming these patterns explicitly helps students self-correct before the exam, not during it.

## Examples

- **Speaking:** A memorised answer to "Tell me about your hometown" that doesn't actually respond to a slightly different follow-up question — examiners notice when an answer doesn't fit the question asked.
- **Writing:** An essay that says "There are many advantages and disadvantages" without ever stating the writer's own opinion, when the question specifically asks for one.
- **Both:** Overusing a small set of "advanced" words incorrectly (e.g. "moreover" used mid-sentence) in an attempt to sound more sophisticated.

## Practice Activity

Give the student a short writing sample or recorded answer (their own from a previous lesson works well). Together, identify one Speaking-pattern mistake and one Writing-pattern mistake from the list above. Rewrite or re-answer with the specific fix applied, then compare the before and after.

## Teacher Tip

Focus feedback on one or two patterns per session rather than every error at once — students retain and apply corrections much better when they aren't overwhelmed with a long list of issues.
