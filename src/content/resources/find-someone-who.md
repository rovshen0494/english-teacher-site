## Learning Objective

Students form questions and use them to gather information from classmates in a natural, low-pressure format.

## Materials

A grid with 6-9 statements such as "has a pet," "likes pizza," "can swim," with space to write a classmate's name next to each.

## Preparation

Prepare a grid matching recently taught vocabulary or grammar (e.g. "can" for ability, "like" for preferences).

## How It Works

1. Review how to turn each grid statement into a question, e.g. "has a pet" becomes "Do you have a pet?"
2. Give each student a grid and set a time limit to mingle and ask classmates.
3. Students may only write a name in a box after that classmate has answered "yes."
4. A classmate's name can only be used once per grid, encouraging students to speak to many different people.
5. Bring the class back together and ask a few students to share what they found out.

## Student Instructions

Walk around and ask your classmates the questions. If they say "yes," write their name in the box. Try to fill the whole grid!

## Variations

**Easier:** Reduce to 4-5 simple statements and pre-write the questions on the board.

**Harder:** Add a follow-up question requirement, e.g. if someone says yes to "likes pizza," ask "What is your favourite pizza?"

## Extension Activity

In pairs, students compare grids and tell each other one interesting thing they learned about the class.

## Teacher Tip

This format is especially useful for quieter students, since each conversation is short, structured and low-stakes.
