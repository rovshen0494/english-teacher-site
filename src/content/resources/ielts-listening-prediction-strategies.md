## Explanation

Before each IELTS Listening section plays, there is time to read the questions. Strong candidates use this time to predict what kind of answer is needed (a number, a name, a type of place) and to notice likely distractors — moments where the speaker mentions something similar before giving the real answer. Predicting the answer type in advance makes it much easier to catch the correct information the first time, since there is no replay.

## Examples

Question: "The meeting will take place at ______." → Predict: a place name is needed, likely 1-3 words.

Common distractor pattern: the speaker says "We were going to meet at the library, but actually it's been moved to the community hall" — the first location mentioned is not the answer.

## Practice Activity

Give the student a set of Listening-style gapped questions without audio. For each gap, ask them to predict: what type of word is missing (number, name, place, time)? Then discuss what kind of distractor language might appear before the real answer, based on the sentence structure.

## Teacher Tip

Even without audio, practising prediction alone builds a habit that transfers directly to real listening tests — many students lose marks not from mishearing, but from not knowing what kind of answer to listen for in the first place.
