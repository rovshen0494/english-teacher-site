## Learning Objective

Students negotiate and justify opinions in English while working toward a group decision.

## Materials

A list of 6-8 items related to a single theme (e.g. "most important inventions," "biggest environmental problems").

## Preparation

Choose a ranking topic relevant to the class and prepare the list of items.

## How It Works

1. Present the list and the ranking task: order the items from most to least important, with group agreement.
2. Put students into small groups of 3-4 and give them time to discuss and agree on an order.
3. Encourage the use of negotiation language: "I think... is more important because...", "I disagree, because..."
4. Each group presents their final ranking and one key reason for their top choice.
5. Compare rankings across groups and discuss any major differences as a class.

## Student Instructions

Work with your group to agree on an order. Everyone must agree before you finalise your ranking!

## Variations

**Easier:** Reduce to 4-5 items and provide sentence starters for agreeing and disagreeing.

**Harder:** Require groups to reach unanimous agreement rather than a majority vote.

## Extension Activity

Individually, students write a short paragraph defending their personal top choice, even if it differs from their group's final ranking.

## Teacher Tip

Choose a topic with genuinely debatable items — if the "correct" order feels too obvious, the negotiation language won't get real practice.
