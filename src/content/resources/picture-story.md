## Learning Objective

Students use time-order language and simple past or present tense to retell a story from picture cues.

## Materials

Sets of 4-6 picture cards showing a simple everyday sequence (e.g. waking up, getting dressed, eating breakfast, going to school).

## Preparation

Shuffle each set of cards so the sequence is out of order before handing them to pairs or groups.

## How It Works

1. Give each pair or small group a shuffled set of picture cards.
2. Students work together to arrange the cards into a logical order.
3. Model useful sequencing language first: "First... Then... After that... Finally..."
4. Once ordered, each group tells their story aloud to the class or to another group, one sentence per picture.
5. Check as a class whether all groups arrived at a similar, logical order.

## Student Instructions

Put the pictures in the right order. Then tell the story using "First, Then, After that, Finally."

## Variations

**Easier:** Pre-teach the key vocabulary in each picture before starting, and reduce to 4 cards.

**Harder:** Ask students to add one extra detail sentence to each picture (who, where, or how they feel).

## Extension Activity

Students draw one new picture to add to the sequence and explain where it fits and why.

## Teacher Tip

Choose a sequence with only one logical order — ambiguous sequences make the follow-up discussion harder to manage productively.
