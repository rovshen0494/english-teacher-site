## Learning Objective

Students construct accurate yes/no questions and use logical elimination to narrow down an answer.

## Materials

None — only a category to think within (person, place, animal, or object).

## Preparation

Decide a category in advance and think of a specific answer within it.

## How It Works

1. Tell students the category (e.g. "a famous person") and that you are thinking of something specific within it.
2. Students take turns asking yes/no questions to gather clues.
3. Keep a visible tally of questions asked, up to a maximum of twenty.
4. Students may guess the answer directly at any point, but a wrong guess still counts as one question.
5. Reveal the answer once someone guesses correctly or the question limit is reached, then start a new round.

## Student Instructions

Ask yes/no questions to figure out the secret answer. You only have twenty questions, so make them count!

## Variations

**Easier:** Reduce the category to a smaller, visible set of options (e.g. 8 pictures on the board).

**Harder:** Lower the question limit to ten to encourage more strategic questioning.

## Extension Activity

In pairs, students play a round themselves, with one student choosing a secret answer for their partner to guess.

## Teacher Tip

Model two or three strong opening questions first ("Is it a person? Is it alive?") — students often start with overly specific questions that waste early turns.
