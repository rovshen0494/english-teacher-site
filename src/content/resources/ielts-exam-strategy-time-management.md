## Explanation

A well-prepared student can still lose marks simply from poor time management — spending too long perfecting one Reading question, or writing a beautiful introduction and running out of time for the Task 2 conclusion. Every section benefits from a rough internal time budget, decided in advance and practised until it feels automatic rather than stressful.

## Examples

- **Writing:** roughly 20 minutes for Task 1, 40 minutes for Task 2 (Task 2 is worth more, so it deserves more time).
- **Reading:** roughly 20 minutes per passage, moving on from a difficult question rather than losing minutes on it.
- **Task 2 breakdown:** 5 minutes planning, 30 minutes writing, 5 minutes checking — checking is often skipped entirely under pressure, but it catches real, fixable errors.

## Practice Activity

Give the student a full Task 2 question under real time pressure (40 minutes). Insert a visible timer with the checkpoints above (5 / 35 / 40 minutes) so they can see whether their pacing matches the plan. Review afterward: which stage took longer than planned, and why?

## Teacher Tip

Timing habits formed in relaxed practice rarely hold up under real exam pressure. Run at least two or three fully timed practice sessions before the exam so the pacing becomes second nature rather than a new stress on the day.
