## Explanation

In Part 2, candidates get one minute to prepare notes on a cue card topic, then speak for one to two minutes without interruption. The most common problem isn't a lack of ideas — it's running out of things to say after 40 seconds. A simple four-point note structure during preparation prevents this: describe it, say when/where, explain how or why, and add a personal reflection or feeling.

## Examples

Cue card: *Describe a skill you would like to learn.*

Sample notes using the four-point structure:
- **What:** learning to cook Vietnamese food properly
- **When/where:** started thinking about it after moving to a new city
- **How/why:** watching a family member cook, wanting to be more independent
- **Feeling:** would feel proud, also a way to connect with local culture

These four short notes are enough to generate 90+ seconds of natural, connected speech.

## Practice Activity

Give the student a cue card. Time exactly one minute of silent note-taking using the four-point structure above. Then time exactly two minutes of speaking, without interrupting. Afterward, review together: did they use the full two minutes? Did they cover all four points?

## Teacher Tip

Resist correcting grammar mid-speech. Let the two minutes run uninterrupted every time — timing discipline and fluency come from practising the real exam conditions, not from being stopped and corrected constantly.
