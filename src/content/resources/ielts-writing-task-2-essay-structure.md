## Explanation

Task 2 asks for a formal essay of at least 250 words responding to a viewpoint, argument or problem. Under 40 minutes of exam pressure, students who plan almost always outperform those who start writing immediately. A dependable structure is: introduction (paraphrase + clear thesis), body paragraph 1 (one main idea with an example), body paragraph 2 (a second main idea with an example), and a conclusion that restates the position.

## Examples

Thesis sentence for the question "Some people think technology has made communication less personal. Discuss both views and give your opinion":

"While some argue that digital communication has reduced genuine personal connection, this essay will argue that technology has, on balance, made communication more accessible without necessarily making it less personal."

Each body paragraph should open with a clear topic sentence stating its single main idea before any example is given.

## Practice Activity

Give the student a Task 2 question and five minutes only to plan: a one-sentence thesis and one main idea for each of two body paragraphs, written as notes, not full sentences. Check the plan alone before any writing begins — a strong plan should make the essay's structure predictable before a single full sentence is written.

## Teacher Tip

Enforce the five-minute planning limit strictly, even in practice. Students who skip planning under time pressure in the real exam are almost always the same students who skipped it in practice — build the habit early.
