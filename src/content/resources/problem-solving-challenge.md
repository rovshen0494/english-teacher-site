## Learning Objective

Students propose, evaluate and agree on solutions to a realistic problem, using suggestion and negotiation language.

## Materials

A short written scenario describing a realistic problem (e.g. "Your school wants to reduce plastic waste but has a limited budget").

## Preparation

Choose or write a scenario with no single obvious correct answer, appropriate to the group's interests and level.

## How It Works

1. Present the scenario and check students understand the situation and constraints.
2. Teach or review suggestion language: "What if we...?", "One option could be...", "I suggest that..."
3. In small groups, students brainstorm possible solutions for a set time.
4. Groups narrow their ideas down to one recommended solution, with reasons.
5. Each group presents their solution to the class; open a short class discussion comparing approaches.

## Student Instructions

Work with your group to suggest solutions, then agree on the best one. Be ready to explain your reasoning.

## Variations

**Easier:** Provide 3-4 possible solutions for groups to discuss and rank, rather than generating their own.

**Harder:** Add a constraint partway through (e.g. "the budget has just been cut in half") that groups must adapt to.

## Extension Activity

Groups write a short summary of their solution as if presenting it to a decision-maker.

## Teacher Tip

Choose scenarios with real stakes for your students' context (school, local community, environment) — genuine relevance produces much richer discussion than abstract hypotheticals.
