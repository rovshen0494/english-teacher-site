## Learning Objective

Students listen to a sequence of clues and use process of elimination to identify an object.

## Materials

One hidden object, or a written list of 5-6 clues about it, ordered from vague to specific.

## Preparation

Choose an object and write 5-6 clues, starting general ("It is used every day") and becoming more specific ("It has buttons and a screen").

## How It Works

1. Tell students you are thinking of an object and will give clues one at a time.
2. Read the first, most general clue aloud.
3. Allow one or two guesses before giving the next clue.
4. Continue through the clues, allowing guesses after each one, until someone identifies the object.
5. Reveal the object (or show it, if hidden nearby) and briefly discuss which clue was most helpful.

## Student Instructions

Listen to each clue. Think about what the object could be before you guess.

## Variations

**Easier:** Show a set of 4 possible objects on the board so students choose rather than guess freely.

**Harder:** Have students work in pairs to write their own 5-clue mystery object for another pair to solve.

## Extension Activity

Once a few rounds are complete, ask students to write down two clues for an object of their own choice as a quick written task.

## Teacher Tip

Use this as a warm-up tied to the lesson's topic — a mystery object related to the upcoming vocabulary set doubles as a lead-in to new material.
