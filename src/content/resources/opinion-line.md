## Learning Objective

Students express degrees of opinion and give reasons, using agreement and disagreement language.

## Materials

None required, though marking the two ends of the room ("Strongly Agree" / "Strongly Disagree") helps.

## Preparation

Prepare 5-6 statements likely to generate a range of opinions (e.g. "Smartphones do more good than harm").

## How It Works

1. Explain the two ends of the room represent "Strongly Agree" and "Strongly Disagree," with the middle as neutral.
2. Read a statement aloud and ask students to stand at the point on the line that matches their opinion.
3. Ask a few students at different points to explain their position briefly.
4. Allow students to move if someone else's reasoning changes their mind.
5. Repeat with the next statement, encouraging different students to speak each round.

## Student Instructions

Stand where your opinion fits on the line. Be ready to explain why — and you can move if you change your mind!

## Variations

**Easier:** Simplify to just two positions (agree/disagree) rather than a full spectrum.

**Harder:** Require students to respond directly to the previous speaker's point before giving their own.

## Extension Activity

Students write a short paragraph explaining their position on one statement from the lesson.

## Teacher Tip

This activity works well for quieter students too, since standing in a position is a valid form of participation even before anyone speaks.
