## Learning Objective

Students plan and deliver a short, structured presentation, using signposting language to guide their audience.

## Materials

A list of topic choices matched to student interests, and a simple structure guide (introduction, 2-3 main points, conclusion).

## Preparation

Prepare a short list of topics students can choose from, or allow them to propose their own with approval.

## How It Works

1. Introduce the structure: a brief introduction, two or three main points, and a short conclusion.
2. Teach signposting language: "Today I'm going to talk about...", "My first point is...", "To sum up..."
3. Give students time to plan their presentation using brief notes, not a full script.
4. Students present individually to a small group or the whole class, within a set time limit (1-2 minutes).
5. After each presentation, the audience asks one genuine question.

## Student Instructions

Plan your presentation using notes, not a full script. Use your structure: introduction, main points, conclusion.

## Variations

**Easier:** Reduce to one main point and allow presenting to a partner rather than the whole class.

**Harder:** Add a requirement to answer two follow-up questions from the audience without preparation.

## Extension Activity

Students give each other one piece of specific, positive feedback and one suggestion for improvement.

## Teacher Tip

Allowing notes rather than a full script keeps the focus on spoken fluency rather than reading aloud — this produces much more natural delivery.
