## Explanation

IELTS topics repeat across Speaking and Writing: environment, technology, education, health, work. Rather than memorising long word lists, it's more effective to build a small "topic map" of 8-10 genuinely useful words and phrases per theme, each learned with its typical collocation — how it's actually used, not just its dictionary meaning.

## Examples

Topic: Environment

- **renewable energy** (collocation: "invest in renewable energy")
- **carbon footprint** ("reduce your carbon footprint")
- **sustainable** ("a more sustainable lifestyle")
- **single-use plastic** ("cut down on single-use plastic")

Each phrase is learned as a chunk, ready to use directly in a sentence, rather than as a single translated word.

## Practice Activity

Choose one IELTS topic. Together, build a map of 8 words/phrases with their common collocations. Then ask the student to use four of them in original spoken sentences about their own opinions, and the other four in a short written paragraph.

## Teacher Tip

Revisit each topic map briefly in a later lesson rather than moving on permanently — spaced repetition across two or three sessions makes topic vocabulary far more likely to appear naturally in the actual exam.
