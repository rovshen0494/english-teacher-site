## Explanation

Part 3 moves from the personal topics of Part 1 and 2 into broader, more abstract discussion connected to the Part 2 topic. Examiners are assessing the ability to discuss ideas, compare, speculate and justify opinions — not just describe. A reliable structure is: give a clear opinion, explain the reason, then add an example or a contrasting view to show depth.

## Examples

**Question:** Do you think technology has made people less social?

Structured answer: "I'd say it's had a mixed effect, to be honest. On one hand, technology can reduce face-to-face contact — people might message instead of visiting someone. But on the other hand, it's also created new ways to stay connected, especially with people who live far away. So I don't think it's made people less social exactly, just social in a different way."

This answer gives a clear stance, a reason, and a contrasting point — exactly what Part 3 rewards.

## Practice Activity

Ask the student three Part 3 style questions connected by a theme (e.g. technology, education, environment). For each answer, check for three things: a clear opinion, a reason, and either an example or a contrasting idea. If any element is missing, ask a natural follow-up question to draw it out.

## Teacher Tip

Teach a small set of flexible linking phrases — "on one hand... on the other hand," "that said," "to some extent" — these single-handedly make Part 3 answers sound noticeably more sophisticated and organised.
