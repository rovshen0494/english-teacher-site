## Learning Objective

Students discuss a realistic problem in depth, using reasoning and suggestion language over an extended conversation.

## Materials

A short scenario relevant to adult life (e.g. "A friend asks to borrow a large sum of money — how do you respond?").

## Preparation

Choose a scenario with genuine complexity and no single correct answer.

## How It Works

1. Present the scenario and check comprehension with a couple of clarifying questions.
2. Give students a minute to think individually before discussing.
3. In small groups, students discuss possible responses and reasoning, aiming for at least 8-10 minutes of sustained talk.
4. Circulate and note useful language or recurring errors without interrupting the flow.
5. Bring the class together to compare different groups' conclusions and reasoning.

## Student Instructions

Discuss the situation with your group. Explain your reasoning, and listen carefully to other people's views.

## Variations

**Easier:** Provide a short list of possible responses as discussion starters.

**Harder:** Add a follow-up complication partway through the discussion to keep the conversation developing.

## Extension Activity

Students write a short reflection on which argument from the discussion they found most convincing and why.

## Teacher Tip

Resist the urge to correct errors during the discussion itself — save language feedback for after, so the conversation stays natural and uninterrupted.
