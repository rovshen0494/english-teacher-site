Adult learners studying English for work rarely lack grammar knowledge — many have studied English formally for years. What they often lack is practice actually using it in real time, in the specific situations they'll face at work: a meeting update, a difficult client conversation, small talk with a new colleague.

Conversation-based lessons close that gap far more effectively than another round of grammar exercises.

## Start from real situations, not textbook topics

Generic role-play topics ("at the restaurant," "at the airport") are useful early on, but adult professionals get far more value from scenarios drawn from their actual work. A **Business Conversation Challenge** built around real situations — requesting a deadline extension, giving a project update, softening disagreement politely — gives students language they can use the next day, not just in the next lesson.

## Build fluency through repetition, not correction

**Speed Networking**, where students have several short timed conversations with different partners, works well precisely because of the repetition. Most adult learners visibly loosen up by their third or fourth conversation — not because the grammar improved, but because the same functional language has now been used several times in slightly different contexts.

## Teach vocabulary the way it's actually used

Isolated word lists rarely transfer to real conversation. Teaching vocabulary **in context** — through a short text or set of example sentences showing natural collocations — helps students absorb not just what a word means, but how it's actually used alongside other words.

## Let discussion run without constant correction

Adult learners often already have strong opinions and reasoning skills in their first language — the goal in a **Problem-Solving Discussion** is to let that reasoning come through in English, uninterrupted, before circling back to language feedback afterward. Constant mid-conversation correction breaks fluency and confidence far more than it builds accuracy.

## The bigger picture

Business English doesn't need to feel separate from "real" English. The most effective lessons simply take the conversations adult learners are already having — or will soon have — and give them a safe space to practise them first.
