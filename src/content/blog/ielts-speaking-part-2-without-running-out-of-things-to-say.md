Ask most IELTS candidates what worries them about Speaking Part 2, and the answer usually isn't grammar or pronunciation — it's the fear of running out of things to say and sitting in silence before the two minutes are up. The good news is that this is almost always a planning problem, not a language problem, and it has a reliable fix.

## The real issue: not enough ideas, not enough English

Candidates are given one minute to prepare notes before speaking for one to two minutes on a cue card topic. Many use that minute to think of vocabulary or worry about grammar — and then discover, thirty seconds into their answer, that they've already said everything they planned.

The fix isn't more vocabulary. It's a simple structure that generates enough content to fill the full two minutes, every time.

## A four-point structure that works

During the one-minute preparation, jot down four short notes:

1. **What** — a brief description of the topic
2. **When/where** — context: when or where it happened, or when it applies
3. **How/why** — some explanation or reasoning
4. **Feeling** — a personal reflection or opinion about it

These four points, each expanded into two or three sentences, comfortably fill the full speaking time without ever feeling padded or repetitive — because each point naturally leads somewhere new.

## Practise the full two minutes, every time

It's tempting to stop a practice answer early once the ideas seem to be running dry. Resist that instinct. Candidates need to practise finding a way to extend an answer under real time pressure, because that is exactly what will happen in the exam. Letting an answer trail off in practice just reinforces the exact problem you're trying to fix.

## Timing builds confidence

Once a student can reliably speak for the full two minutes using the four-point structure, the anxiety around Part 2 tends to drop significantly — not because the language got easier, but because they now have a repeatable plan they trust, whatever the topic turns out to be.
